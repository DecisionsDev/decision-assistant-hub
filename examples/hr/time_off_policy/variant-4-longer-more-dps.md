# Acme Corp Time Off Policy

Acme Corp provides multiple types of time off for employees, including holidays,
vacation, and sick leave. Eligibility is typically limited to regular full-time
employees unless stated otherwise.

---

## 1. Holidays

### 1.1 Fixed Holidays

Only regular full-time employees are entitled to paid time off under this policy.
Part-time, temporary, and contract workers are not covered unless a separate
written agreement states otherwise. Before any holiday entitlement is assessed,
the employee's classification must be confirmed as regular full-time.

Even within the broader category of full-time workers, supplemental employees
occupy a distinct classification and are excluded from several entitlements.
Supplemental status must be verified before any holiday benefit is applied.

Acme Corp observes the following eight paid fixed holidays each calendar year for
all eligible regular full-time employees:

* New Year's Day
* Martin Luther King Jr. Day
* Memorial Day
* Independence Day
* Labor Day
* Thanksgiving Day
* Day after Thanksgiving
* Christmas Day

These dates are predetermined and require no individual request or manager action.
An eligible employee who works on one of these days is entitled to alternative
compensation as outlined in the Compensation Policy; however, simply being an
eligible employee on that date is sufficient to receive the paid holiday benefit.

### 1.2 Personal Choice Holidays

Regular full-time employees who are not supplemental employees are eligible for
up to four (4) personal choice holidays per calendar year. These may be used to
observe additional holidays of the employee's choosing — religious observances,
cultural celebrations, or any other personally significant day — subject to the
conditions below.

Use of a personal choice holiday is subject to manager approval. Employees must
submit a request in advance using the standard time-off request system. The
manager will evaluate the request in light of team capacity and business needs
before granting approval. Approval is not automatic; however, managers are
expected to accommodate requests wherever operationally feasible.

### Carry-Over of Unused Personal Choice Holidays

All unused personal choice holidays expire on December 31 of the year in
which they were granted. Personal choice holidays are a use-it-or-lose-it benefit
and may not be rolled forward, banked, paid out upon termination, or applied to a
future year under any circumstances. Employees are therefore encouraged to
schedule their personal choice holidays early in the year to avoid forfeiture.

*To illustrate:* an employee granted four personal choice holidays on January 1
who uses only two of them by December 15 cannot carry the remaining two days into
the new year. Those two days expire at midnight on December 31, and the employee's
new annual allotment of four days becomes available on January 1 of the following
year.

## 2. Vacation

### 2.1 Vacation Entitlement Based on Years of Service

The number of vacation days an employee receives each year is determined by their
length of continuous service with Acme Corp, evaluated at the start of each
calendar year (or at the date of hire for new employees — see the proration section
below).

Employees with fewer than 10 full years of continuous service receive **15 working
days (3 weeks)** of paid vacation per year.

Employees who have completed 10 or more full years of continuous service receive
**20 working days (4 weeks)** of paid vacation per year.

Employees who satisfy both of the following conditions — a hire date prior to
January 1, 2004, and 20 or more years of continuous service — receive **25 working
days (5 weeks)** of paid vacation per year. This tier is no longer available to
employees hired on or after January 1, 2004.

| Years of Service | Vacation Days per Year |
|---|---|
| Fewer than 10 years | 15 working days (3 weeks) |
| 10 years or more | 20 working days (4 weeks) |
| Hired before Jan 1, 2004, AND 20+ years of service | 25 working days (5 weeks) |

### 2.2 Mid-Year Hire Proration

When an employee joins Acme Corp on any date other than January 1, their vacation
entitlement for the hire year is prorated to reflect only the portion of the year
during which they are employed. Proration is calculated as follows:

> **Prorated entitlement = annual entitlement × (full calendar months remaining
> in the hire year ÷ 12), rounded down to the nearest half-day.**

"Full calendar months remaining" means complete months from the first day of the
month following the hire date through December 31. The month in which the
employee is hired is not counted unless the hire date is the first calendar day of
that month.

*For instance:* an employee hired on March 10 in their first year (annual
entitlement of 15 days) has 9 full calendar months remaining (April through
December). Their prorated entitlement is 15 × 9/12 = 11.25 days, which rounds
down to 11 days (since 0.25 is less than 0.5). Had the hire date been March 1,
the month of March would count, giving 10 full months remaining and a prorated
entitlement of 15 × 10/12 = 12.5 days, which is already a half-day multiple and
requires no further rounding.

Starting January 1 of the following calendar year, the employee receives their
full annual entitlement as determined by their years-of-service tier.

### 2.3 Vacation Usage Conditions

Vacation may be taken in half-day, full-day, or multi-day increments. No
increment smaller than a half-day is permitted. Employees planning an absence of
four or more consecutive vacation days are encouraged to notify their manager at
least two weeks in advance.

All vacation requests require prior manager approval submitted through the
standard time-off request system. Requests are generally evaluated on a
first-come, first-served basis; however, seniority may be applied as a tiebreaker
during peak periods at the manager's discretion.

Manager approval is conditioned on the absence not creating an unreasonable
operational burden. Managers must document the business justification for any
denial and are expected to propose alternative dates wherever practical.

While business needs take precedence, managers must give reasonable weight to the
employee's stated preference — including personal obligations, travel commitments,
and previously approved plans — when evaluating competing requests. The goal is to
reach a mutually acceptable schedule that serves both the team and the individual.

## 3. Personal Sick Days

### 3.1 Sick Day Entitlement

All regular full-time employees receive **6 days of paid sick leave per calendar
year**. Sick days may be used for the employee's own illness, injury, or
medical appointment. Sick days do not carry over to the following year and are not
paid out upon termination.

### 3.2 Use of Sick Days for a Family Member's Illness

Employees may use **up to 3 of their 6 annual sick days** to care for an ill or
injured family member — for example, a spouse, domestic partner, child, or parent.
The **remaining 3 sick days are reserved exclusively for the employee's own illness
or medical needs** and may not be redirected to family care, even if the employee
believes they will not need them for personal use during that year.

For the purposes of this rule, "family member" means a person who resides in the
employee's household or with whom the employee has a documented caretaking
relationship. Human Resources maintains a non-exhaustive list of qualifying
relationships; employees with questions should consult HR before taking family
sick leave.

*As an example:* an employee who uses 2 sick days to stay home with a sick child
has 1 family sick day remaining and 3 personal sick days intact. If that employee
later needs 4 days off for their own surgery, they may use all 3 personal sick
days but may not convert the unused family sick day to personal use, resulting in
1 unpaid day (absent another applicable leave program). Conversely, if the
employee never uses family sick days, those 3 days simply expire on December 31
alongside any unused personal sick days.
